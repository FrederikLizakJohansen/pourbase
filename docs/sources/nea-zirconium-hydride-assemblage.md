# Zirconium hydride: original-source and assemblage review

The NEA02g3a source review confirms that the held **ZrH bulk correlation**
needs an assemblage or solid-solution representation. All three NEA values
remain saved in the external candidate ledger. The release database stays
at **989 species and 3,145 observations**, with every preferred value
preserved. The existing ε-ZrH2 phase retains its identity and observations.

## What the original measurements establish

Douglas and Victor (1958), *Heat Content of Zirconium and of Five Compositions
of Zirconium Hydride from 0° to 900°C*, Journal of Research of the NBS 61,
13–23, [DOI 10.6028/jres.061.002](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.061.002),
measured relative heat contents by drop calorimetry. Table 1, p. 13, gives
H/Zr ratios 0.324, 0.556, 0.701, 0.999 and 1.071. These are atomic ratios;
the corresponding hydrogen contents span about 24–52 **atomic percent**.
The discussion on p. 20 identifies the samples as differing proportions of
the same two phases in the low-temperature region. Heat contents are
relative to 0°C; they cannot directly supply standard formation enthalpies.
The nearly unit bulk ratio establishes no separate stoichiometric ZrH phase.

Douglas (1963), *High-Temperature Thermodynamic Functions for Zirconium and
Unsaturated Zirconium Hydrides*, Journal of Research of the NBS 67A,
403–426, [DOI 10.6028/jres.067A.043](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.067A.043),
provides a later thermodynamic correlation. Its conventions on p. 404 define
one mole as one mole of Zr plus x moles of H, including multiphase samples.
Its calorie is 4.184 J and its hydrogen-gas reference is 1 atm.
Table 13, p. 424, explicitly labels bulk ZrH1.00 at 298.15 K as **α + δ**.
Its entropy, 9.16 cal/(mol K), converts to 38.32544 J/(mol K), close to the
NEA bulk value. This agreement concerns bulk thermochemistry.

The NBS evaluation is a fitted, internally assessed model with documented
experimental inconsistencies. Its historical phase symbols retain their
source meanings. No assignment to a distinct modern γ-ZrH polymorph is
made here. The original full 1958 JACS correlation
([DOI 10.1021/ja01552a010](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja01552a010)) and Turnbull's
1964 article ([DOI 10.1071/CH9641063](https://doi.org/10.1071/CH9641063))
remain retrieval targets: publisher metadata were verified, while direct
full-text retrieval failed. Secondary copies supply no new imported numbers.

## Phase fractions and the calculation model

Equation 16 of the 1963 study expresses a bulk property as the lever-rule
sum of terminal-phase properties. For bulk H/Zr ratio x and terminal ratios
xα and xδ, the fractions on a Zr-mole basis are

`fδ = (x − xα) / (xδ − xα)` and `fα = 1 − fδ`.

At the source's 820 K eutectoid, Table 4 and equation 102 give xα = 0.065
and xδ = 1.171. Choosing the α + δ side at x = 1 gives fα = 0.154611 and
fδ = 0.845389. These fractions conserve Zr and H. They are neither mass
fractions nor room-temperature fractions. Three phases can coexist at the
eutectoid; the example selects its α + δ side.

At 298.15 K, Table 4 gives two α-boundary estimates, 0.000015 and 0.0006,
and discusses their difference. The source tables use the latter for thermal
consistency. A consistent room-temperature δ endpoint and its properties
must accompany whichever boundary evaluation is adopted. The 820 K example
supplies no room-temperature endmember set.

Current pourbase condensed species have fixed formula units and unit activity.
Admitting a bulk mixture as an independent solid would give it an artificial
phase field. As a diagnostic, the held NEA ZrH G lies 2,545.5 J/mol below the
simple half-Zr/half-ε-ZrH2 chord. That chord uses different endpoints from the
source assemblage and supplies no valid replacement model. Both copying the
bulk value to a pure ZrH phase and decomposing it into the existing endpoints
remain scientifically unsupported.

## Retained NEA values and a source-unit discrepancy

NEA volume 8, Table III-1, printed p. 48/PDF p. 78, reports:

| Bulk composition | G, J/mol | H, J/mol | S, J/(mol K) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ZrH | −64686 ± 801 | −84400 ± 800 | 38.3 ± 0.1 |

All three remain in the external ledger with their 298.15 K, 1 bar conditions,
source uncertainty and OECD reuse basis. Using the review's elemental
entropies reconstructs G = −64686.322 J/mol, within source rounding.

The page image for p. 132 confirms that equation V.22 gives
`ΔfH(ZrHx) = −84.4 x kJ/mol`, followed by a comparison labelled
−42.2 kJ per mole H2. The slope instead implies −168.8 kJ for uptake of one
mole H2, since that adds two moles of H atoms. The printed comparison differs
by a factor of four. This is a recorded internal unit/basis discrepancy;
no official correction or repair of the selected table is asserted.

## Reuse, release state and remaining work

The original NBS studies are separate from NIST SRD products. The
[article-specific rights notice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5319804/)
identifies the Journal of Research papers as public domain in the United
States, with separate rights possible for third-party illustrations.
[NIST's publication and data terms](https://www.nist.gov/open/license) were
also recorded. Source PDFs, page images, retrieval responses and hashes stay
in the external review workspace. The distributed review uses our prose and
cited numerical facts; no source figure is reproduced.

Completed NEA permission checks carry forward. OECD and NEA acknowledgment,
the same requirement in sublicenses, and onward propagation to further
sublicensees remain applicable. See [the notices](https://github.com/FrederikLizakJohansen/pourbase/blob/main/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
The hydride admission hold concerns physical representation; other sources'
permission holds retain their independent status.

The original-source checkpoint is complete. NEA02g3a1 remains open for a
validated solution/assemblage representation, compatible terminal phases at
298.15 K, pressure conventions and uncertainty treatment. Full methods of
the remaining original articles can inform that work. The calcium formula
hold, three 323.15 K Gibbs calculation holds and gas thermal reassessment
remain separately tracked. This checkpoint adds **zero observations, zero
species and zero newly available properties** to the release database.
