## Complete formulas and buffered components

Existing PO4³⁻, P2O7⁴⁻ and AsO4³⁻ references represent the admitted
formulas. Hydration waters occur once in the core O/H counts. Vivianite keeps
three Fe atoms, two phosphates and eight waters. Fe4(AsO4)4·3H2O keeps four
Fe atoms, four arsenates and three waters; its published G/H/S are already per
that complete unit. Per-Fe calculations divide by the primary count once.

FeHPO4(aq) and FeH2PO4⁺ retain one and two core H atoms. Their balanced
formation reactions consume one and two protons respectively, and release two
electrons per Fe. Fe3PO7 keeps three core O atoms beyond PO4³⁻ and releases
three electrons per Fe. The mixed-valence pyrophosphate is explicitly
Fe(II)Fe(III)2(P2O7)2 in the source; its formal mean and reaction coefficient
are 8/3 per Fe. Named hydrates and polymorphs remain separate. Exact specimen
space groups are unassigned; generic crystalline H/S-only phases retain
unresolved polymorph qualifications.

These are externally prescribed component activities. Closed balances conserve
the primary Fe inventory; they do not impose closed P/As totals, protonation
balances or a new coupled phosphate/arsenate inventory model. H/S-only records
retain missing G and stay outside Gibbs-energy diagrams. The current thermal
model keeps its existing ligand exclusions.

## Methods, estimates and phase qualifications

The aqueous complexes and vivianite use NEA's critically evaluated low-ionic-
strength solubility/speciation results, with deliberately enlarged uncertainty.
The complexes' stoichiometries are model identifications with limited direct
spectroscopic support. Trigonal FePO4 uses the review's oxide-melt calorimetric
reanalysis and accepted entropy integration. Scorodite and parascorodite H use
own acid-solution cycle recalculations. The ferric arsenate trihydrate uses own
solubility evaluation and calorimetry; its H is the source's internally calculated
property from selected G/S. No additional local H or uncertainty is fabricated.

Strengite S =174 ±7 J/(mol K) is marked `source_verified_estimate`. The
review combines measured heat capacities with a speculative contribution from
an unmeasured low-temperature magnetic peak (pp. 241, 244). Its H/G remain
absent. Other accepted entropy integrations retain their experimental methods.
The source reference identifiers are `2013SHI/ZHA` for Fe2P2O7,
`2013SHI/ZHA2` for trigonal FePO4 and mixed-valence Fe3(P2O7)2, and
`2013SHI/ZHA3` for Fe3PO7 and Fe4(P2O7)3 (Appendix A, pp. 632–633).
The source family class A identifies the critical review; each property's
scientific status records its method.

Kaňkite's reported G/H/S remain on a nominal specimen record with unresolved
phase history. NEA p. 264 reports later concern about spontaneous or cryogenic
conversion to layered FeAsO4·2H2O. The original specimen was X-rayed only
at the start of calorimetry. Three exact policy exclusions keep these reported
values outside recommendations until the sample-to-phase correspondence is
reconciled. This scientific question is separate from reuse permission.

The two monoclinic Fe(PO3)3 entropy rows remain external. Their condensed
phosphate formulas contain fewer O atoms than the existing PO4/P2O7 buffers.
The current model requires nonnegative core O/H counts. An invented PO3
reference or a wrong formula would obscure that limitation. The anhydrous
210 ±3 result also uses an own water-removal estimate; the 0.128-water
specimen's 215.4 ±2.37 result is calorimetric. Both source values and
locations remain in the ledger for a compatible representation.
